Delhi’s GRAP,stage by stage

Stage I is in force. What each stage triggers, what the September revision changed, and what the plan cannot do.

By Pawan Kumar Beniwal · 3 min read

Every October, the same question reaches me from friends and family in the NCR: what does GRAP actually do? This year the question is timely. On 21 October the Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) put Delhi-NCR under Stage I after the city’s average AQI reached 244 at 2 pm and 245 at 4 pm, according to Business Today, citing CPCB readings.

GRAP is the Graded Response Action Plan. It is a ladder of emergency measures, and each rung switches on at a higher AQI.

The four stages

Under the CAQM schedule, revision 29.09.2026, the stages are Stage I (‘Poor’, AQI 201–300), Stage II (‘Very Poor’, 301–400), Stage III (‘Severe’, 401–450) and Stage IV (‘Severe +’, above 450). Two details are often missed. Actions are invoked in advance, on the weather and AQI forecast from IMD and IITM, so a stage can start before the AQI gets there. And a higher stage adds to the earlier ones instead of replacing them.

In plain terms, Stage I is mostly dust, burning and smoke: construction dust rules, open-burning bans, no coal or firewood in tandoors, and limits on diesel generators. Stage II adds daily mechanical sweeping, higher parking fees and staggered office timings. Stage III restricts older BS-III petrol and BS-IV diesel cars in Delhi and the listed NCR districts, and moves primary schools to hybrid mode. Stage IV stops most heavy goods vehicles from entering Delhi and halts construction on linear public projects such as roads and pipelines.

What changed in the 2026 revision

The Commission approved the revised schedule at its 30th meeting on 28 September, as the PIB release describes. Some changes are structural. Repeated Stage I actions on dust, municipal waste and transport were merged, and actions on diesel generator sets and BS-IV and older buses from outside Delhi moved up from Stage II to Stage I. That is why Stage I now carries 25 of the plan’s 48 actions (8 in Stage II, 10 in Stage III, 5 in Stage IV).

Others are about enforcement. The BS-III petrol and BS-IV diesel car restrictions now also cover Sonipat, and the exemption for older goods vehicles carrying essential commodities was removed in Stages III and IV. The PIB release does not give the earlier action counts, so I cannot tell you how much the Stage I count grew.

What GRAP can and cannot do

This part is my reading, not a finding. GRAP is a response to a bad day. It is designed to blunt a spike, and it does not reduce the baseline that causes the spike. It also lands unevenly. A car owner can work from home under Stage III. A goods vehicle driver or a daily-wage construction worker cannot, so Stage III and IV decisions deserve to be paired with support for the people they stop working.

My takeaway

GRAP is the emergency brake, not the engine. Watch the forecast, not just the AQI, and judge the plan by how well the year-round measures reduce the need for it.
