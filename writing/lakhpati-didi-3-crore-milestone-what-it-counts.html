Lakhpati Didi:what does the 3 crore count measure?

The count passed three crore and the next target is six. A closer look at what it measures and where the sources differ.

By Pawan Kumar Beniwal · 4 min read

Few phrases in rural policy travel as far as “Lakhpati Didi”. The number attached to it has travelled fast too: past three crore, with a new target of six crore. Livelihoods and financial inclusion are part of my work, so I wanted to read the figures closely before repeating them.

What counts as a Lakhpati Didi

PIB’s explainer defines her as a self-help group member whose household earns Rs 1,00,000 or more a year. That income is calculated over at least four agricultural seasons or business cycles, with average monthly income above Rs 10,000. So it is an income outcome measured over time, not a loan or an enrolment. It also sits inside the rural livelihoods mission (DAY-NRLM). A July 2025 Lok Sabha reply says no separate budget is allocated to the initiative.

How the count moved

In August 2024 PIB said more than one crore women had become Lakhpati Didis, against a target of three crore. A Lok Sabha reply in July 2025 gave 1,48,32,258, from 10.05 crore households organised into 90.90 lakh groups. In February 2026, the Union minister said 3.01 crore women had reached the milestone. A July 2026 PIB release congratulates states on the three crore mark, and in September ANI reported “approximately 3.5 crore”.

Two things do not line up, and I want to say so plainly. The February report says the three crore goal “was achieved by 2025”, yet the July 2025 reply shows 1.48 crore. And the count roughly doubled between July 2025 and February 2026. I could not find a published explanation, such as a change in method or a verification drive. I treat the trend as reported, not audited.

Where the 2025 count sat

In that July 2025 reply, Maharashtra (22,69,981), Andhra Pradesh (17,41,362) and Bihar (14,47,750) together held about 37% of the national total. That share is my own arithmetic from the reply’s state-wise table. Haryana reported 58,577. States differ a lot in how long and how densely self-help groups have operated, so the same target asks different things of different states.

What the next three crore asks

The minister said the six crore target is for 2029-30, adding, “It is difficult compared to the past.” At a review meeting, News On AIR reported he told officials that reaching it would need at least ten crore women linked to the ministry’s financial inclusion initiatives. Other stated goals include Rs 10 lakh crore in group loans over five years.

My reading, and it is only that: the first three crore came where groups were strongest. The next stretch depends on thinner ground, so I would watch three things.

  • State-wise counts, published regularly. A national number hides where the work is hard.
  • Staying power. How many households are still above the line a few years later.
  • One definition. PIB speaks of household income, while the February report says a woman’s net annual income. Which one is verified, and how, matters.

My takeaway

A Lakhpati Didi count tells you how many households crossed an income line. What matters next is how many stay above it, and how openly it is verified.

It is the same lesson I drew from Delhi’s EV subsidy data and from farmers’ choices on wheat and rice: a headline number is a start, and delivery is the detail underneath.
